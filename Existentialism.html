Philosophy

Existentialism is a philosophical and logical doctrine arguing that the meaning, or truth, of statements and concepts should be evaluated solely by their extension (the actual objects, sets, or things they refer to) rather than their intension (the inherent qualities, abstract ideas, or definitions that describe them).

Danish philosopher Søren Kierkegaard (1813–1855) is widely recognized as the "father of existentialism". He revolutionized 19th-century philosophy by shifting the focus away from abstract, objective systems of thought and toward the lived, subjective experience of the "single individual," personal choice, and existential dread.



Søren Kierkegaard books

Fear and Trembling

This foundational text explores the "leap of faith" through the biblical story of Abraham and Isaac, challenging traditional ethics and defining the subjective nature of religious faith.

The Sickness Unto Death

An exploration of the existential and psychological dimensions of despair. Kierkegaard argues that despair is a universal human condition stemming from a failure to align one's true self with God.

Either/Or

A massive two-part masterpiece contrasting the "aesthetic" life of sensory pleasure and detachment with the "ethical" life of duty, choice, and commitment.

The Concept of Anxiety

A brilliant psychological and theological deliberation on angst. It frames anxiety as the "dizziness of freedom"—the profound realization that you are responsible for your own choices.

Concluding Unscientific Postscript to Philosophical Fragments

This dense, highly polemical work contains his most famous existential maxim: "Subjectivity is truth." It argues that objective, scientific facts cannot capture the reality of lived, human existence.
